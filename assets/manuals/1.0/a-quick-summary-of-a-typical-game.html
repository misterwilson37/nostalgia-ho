A Quick Summary of a Typical Game

In a nutshell, the goal of Spaceward Ho! is to conquer the galaxy, or at least most of it. You start out on a populous planet that is perfectly suited for your race. You have just become capable of building your first interstellar space ships. So you build a couple of exploratory ships (we call such ships “scouts”) and send them to nearby stars. Some of the planets at these stars will be more habitable than others.

Once you find a planet that is at least relatively like your home planet, you build a colony ship and go colonize it. Once there, you make the planet as habitable as possible (e.g. build domes to control the temperature) and then use it as a basis for further exploration and colonization. After a number of years the planet’s population will grow to the point that it will actually start making money, just like your home planet. Of course, if a planet is too unlike your home planet, the cost to support the population will always be more than the money made by that population, in which case the planet will never become profitable.

Somewhere along the line, you will run into opposition. Depending on what was specified when the galaxy was created, there will be some number of human and computer players. They’re trying to conquer the galaxy, too. In addition to exploring and colonizing, you’ll need to defend yourself against the more aggressive of these alien races. You can do this by building defensive satellites at your colonies and by building fighters and attacking them — the old “the best defense is a good offense” strategy.

Which brings us to the final element of the game. In addition to building ships and terraforming and mining planets, you can spend money on technology research. The first ships you build are capable of travelling to other stars, but they can’t go far, they’re pretty slow, they can’t attack or defend themselves very well, and they require a lot of metal to build. By spending money on research, you can make it possible to build better ships in the future. And as you might imagine, the better ship wins a battle.

So, you may ask, when’s the game over? Good question. If you’ve killed off everyone else, you’ve probably won. We don’t think there’s a good answer to this question, so we’ve left it up to you. As long as the game’s interesting, keep playing.
