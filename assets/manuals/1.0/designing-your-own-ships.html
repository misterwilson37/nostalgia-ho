Designing your own Ships

As your technology increases, you’ll want to start building more and more advanced ships, using these new technologies. It’s easy. Just choose “Create New Ship Type” from the “Ships” menu. It will bring up the Ship Design dialog. You just move the scroll bars around to set the qualities of the new ship. The rightmost side of the scroll bar is always whatever your current Tech level is.

There are four major classes of ships: Colony Ships, Satellites, Scouts, and Fighters:

Colony Ships

A Colony Ship is any ship that has “Colony Ship” checked in the Create New Ship Type dialog. It is important because it carries people and colonizes new stars for you. Colony Ships are also incredibly expensive both in Money and Metal. Colony Ships cost so much that you’ll want to build very few of them. That, and the fact that miniaturization doesn’t do them much good, will mean that you probably won’t want to use any Mini at all on your Colony Ships. Colony Ships are also the first thing the enemy will shoot at in a battle, so you’ll want to be careful with them, too.

Satellites

Satellites are ships that don’t have engines. That means that either their Speed or their Range is zero. Probably both. Satellites are a cheap, effective means of defending your planets against enemy attack. The problem is they can’t move; they can only defend the planet where they were built.

Scouts and Fighters

Scouts and Fighters are ships with engines that aren’t colony ships. The distinction between scouts and fighters is only in how you use them — there’s no reason why you can’t attack with a scout or explore with a fighter.

What makes a scout a scout? Its name. A scout is any ship whose name begins with the word “Scout,” like “Scout 9” or “Scout Rhino.” The big difference between fighters and scouts is that you can have Spaceward Ho! automatically put each scout that is built into its very own fleet, so it can independently go flying off to explore. You do this by checking “Scouts in New Fleets” in the “Options” menu. Fighters (which you name things like “Fighter 5” and “Death Star”) automatically join the fleets at the stars where they’re built, as long as there is already a fighter of that type in the fleet.

You can split up fighters, join scouts, or move ships around however you want by choosing “Organize Fleets” from the “Ships” menu. See the section on “Organizing Fleets” for details.

Development Cost

It costs more to build the first one of a new ship type than all of the ones that come after it — it takes effort to work the kinks out and do testing. This is especially true with ships with high miniaturization. Just keep in mind that the development cost is a one time thing associated with every new ship type.

Scrapping Fleets And Ship Types

Since, as the game progresses, metal is scarce and out of date ships are useless, you may find that ships you built at the beginning of the game are useless later on. You can actually recoup 75% of the metal from ships located at one of your colonies by either scrapping fleets (select “Scrap Current Fleet” from the “Ships” menu) or by scrapping an entire ship type (click on “Scrap Type” for a specific type in the “List Ship Types...” Dialog).

When you mark a ship type or fleet for scrapping, it doesn’t actually do the scrapping until you end the turn. If you get cold feet before then, you can just go and uncheck the box.
